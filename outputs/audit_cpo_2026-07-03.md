# GMEPay+ — CPO Audit: Does the system deliver what it's supposed to?

**Date:** 3 July 2026
**Lens:** Chief Product Officer. Not "are features built?" (that's the department audits) but **"does the product deliver its core promise to the customer, reliably, and can we prove it?"**

---

## Verdict

**GMEPay+ is building the right product, but it has not proven it *delivers* — and until this week it silently did not.**

The single journey the entire product exists to serve — *a partner's customer scans a foreign QR and pays* — was **broken end-to-end** and no one would have known from the inside. It took a live scan (the Nepal/Fonepay attempt) to surface it, and fixing it took **three separate defects** in a row:

1. The Nepal payment rail wasn't deployed/launched at all (missing from the fleet + a port collision).
2. GMEPay+ wasn't consulted for what the QR *is* — the wallet guessed the currency locally.
3. The final "charge" step rejected **100% of payments** on a header-vs-body technicality (the nonce), silently declining every Nepal payment.

Each was individually small. Collectively they mean the flagship journey **never actually worked** despite the corridor being reported as "built." That is the CPO headline: **you cannot currently trust the "done" column**, because "done" has meant *code merged*, not *journey proven*.

---

## A. What the product is supposed to deliver (the promise)

GMEPay+'s promise to its customer (the **partner** — a wallet/remittance company) is:

> *"Plug in once, and your customers can pay any merchant across our supported countries by QR. We handle routing, FX, funding, settlement and compliance invisibly and correctly. You just see your money and your transactions."*

So the product **is not a UI** the payer sees — the payer lives in the *partner's* app. GMEPay+'s product is: **(1) a reliable integration, (2) money that is always correct, (3) fast time-to-integrate, (4) visibility + support after go-live.** Product quality = those four things. There is no fifth thing hiding a weak core behind a pretty screen.

---

## B. Delivery scorecard — against the promise, not the backlog

| # | What the promise requires | Delivers? | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The flagship journey works end-to-end** (scan → classify → amount → pay → receipt) | 🔴→🟡 | Was broken by 3 defects; fixed in code this week; **still not proven live**. |
| 2 | **Money is always correct** (FX, fees, settlement, ledger tie-out) | 🟡 | Engine is genuinely sophisticated & built; but "code exists ≠ tied to the cent in prod." |
| 3 | **It works *every time*** (reliability is the product) | 🔴 | No cross-service end-to-end test. A trivial contract mismatch shipped and declined every payment. |
| 4 | **Fast time-to-integrate** for a partner | 🔴 | No dev portal / self-serve keys / quickstart. One-engineer-per-partner. |
| 5 | **Partner sees their money & transactions** | 🟢 | Self-service portal, txn detail, statements (CSV/PDF), status trail. Real. |
| 6 | **Post-go-live support** (disputes, help) | 🔴 | No dispute/case management; refund is admin-only; no support-scoped role. |
| 7 | **We can measure whether it's working** (product analytics) | 🟡 | Ops has health/decline signals; **product** funnel (activation time, success-rate per partner/corridor, decline-reason trends) is thin. |

Two greens (visibility, the money *engine*). Everything on the **critical delivery path — the journey itself, its reliability, and getting a partner live — is red.**

---

## C. The systemic finding (the one that matters most)

**The organisation has optimised for feature *breadth* over *proven depth of the one journey that pays the bills.***

- 100+ features were surfaced and built across 7 department audits. Impressive coverage.
- Yet the flagship corridor didn't complete a single payment.
- **Nothing caught it** — not CI, not a smoke test, not a demo, because there is no *golden-path* test that exercises scan→pay across the real services and fails the build when it breaks.

For a payments product this is the deepest possible product risk: **the map says "corridor live," the territory says "declined."** Every "built" claim is now suspect until proven by an executed journey. This is the same "provisioned-not-plumbed" warning the CTO and CEO raised — from the product seat it reads as: *we are shipping the appearance of capability faster than the reality of it.*

---

## D. Who is the user, and the product that's missing

- GME's **customer = the partner.** GME's **product to them = DX + uptime + money-correctness + support.**
- Of those four, **DX (integration) and support are weak**, uptime is *unproven*, correctness is *strong-but-untied*.
- GME has **zero product surface to the actual payer** (they're in the partner's app) — which is *correct* strategically, but it means there is no place to hide a weak core. The core *is* the product.
- The **sandbox + simulators (ZeroPay, Nepal QR, merchant, wallet, FX board, service trace) are a genuine, under-leveraged product asset** — most payment platforms make partners integrate blind. This should be productised into the partner onboarding experience, not left as an internal demo console.

---

## E. What GMEPay+ genuinely gets right (credit where due)

- **Multi-scheme, multi-country routing with QR-classified failover** (ADR-016) is a real product *wedge* — it's the differentiated thing a partner can't easily build themselves.
- **The money model** (double-prefund, two-phase, USD-intermediary FX, 3-tier rate cascade, only-partner-charges-customer) is more rigorous than most fintechs ship.
- **GMEPay+ is now authoritative for QR classification** (fixed this week) — the platform, not the client, decides corridor + currency. That's the correct product boundary.
- **Cloud-agnostic deploy + one-DB-per-service** — the product can be sold on-prem or cloud, widening the addressable market.

---

## F. CPO roadmap call (priority order)

1. **Institute the "golden path" gate — this is #1.** One automated end-to-end test *per live corridor* (scan → classify → amount → pay → receipt → settlement tie-out) that runs against the real services and **must stay green to ship**. This is the single change that would have caught all three of this week's defects. Make *"the flagship journey passes"* the definition of "the product works." Everything else is secondary to this.
2. **Redefine "done" as proven-in-prod, tied-to-the-cent** for any money path. Kill "code merged = done" for the critical journey.
3. **Productise onboarding (turn 1-engineer-per-partner into self-serve):** dev portal, sandbox keys, quickstart, using the simulators you already have. This is the growth unlock and the biggest time-to-value lever.
4. **Close the post-sale loop:** dispute/case management + partner-facing decline-reason transparency + a support-scoped role. Today a partner who has a stuck payment has nowhere to go.
5. **Instrument the product funnel:** per-partner/corridor **payment success rate**, decline-reason trends, and **activation time** (contract → first live payment). You cannot improve delivery you cannot see.

---

## G. One-line summary for the exec team

> *We built a broad, sophisticated payments engine and a real routing wedge — but we shipped the flagship journey broken and had no way to know. Before adding anything, make the one journey that matters provably work and stay working; then make partners able to reach it themselves.*
